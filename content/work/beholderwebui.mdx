## The problem

The selective solder driver publishes a continuous stream of RabbitMQ
messages, but nothing surfaced them to the people who needed to see what was
happening — operators watching the line, and engineers debugging it.

## What I built

BeholderWebUI, an internal monitoring UI in Blazor that subscribes to the
driver's RabbitMQ messages and gives operators and engineers filtering,
inspection, and message publishing against a 5,000-message working set.

<ArchDiagram slug="beholderwebui" />

I built this alongside mentoring a junior developer — pairing on the
RabbitMQ subscription model and the filtering UX together. It's the one
place on this site where that shows up, and for a Software Engineer II role,
it's a real signal: this wasn't just solo output.

## Hard parts

Keeping the UI usable at a 5,000-message working set meant the filtering and
inspection model had to be designed for scan-ability from the start, not
bolted on after the list got slow. **[NEEDS PETER]** — the specific
performance or UX decision that mattered most here.

## Outcome

Operators and engineers use it to see and act on driver traffic that used to
be invisible. Internal only — no public URL, no screenshots, by design.

## What I'd do differently

I'd add saved filter presets — the same three or four filters get rebuilt by
hand by whoever's debugging that day.
